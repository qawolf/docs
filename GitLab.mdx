If your repositories are hosted on GitLab.com or on a GitLab Enterprise Server instance with internet access, this is the recommended setup. QA Wolf listens for the deployments your pipeline creates, and a [deployment trigger](/triggers/global-triggers) decides which flows run for each one.

## Requirements

- Access to a QA Wolf workspace with at least one environment configured
- Maintainer access to the GitLab group

## Connect QA Wolf to GitLab

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a group access token">
    Create a GitLab group access token with the **Maintainer** role and the `api` scope. QA Wolf refuses a lesser token.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable the GitLab integration">
    Paste the token into **Workspace settings → Integrations**. Once connected, QA Wolf can set commit statuses and link runs to GitLab commits.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Emit deployments from your pipeline

A job with the `environment:` keyword creates the deployment on its own, and the webhook fires when the job succeeds. No separate API call is needed.

[Report deployments to QA Wolf](/triggers/report-deployments#gitlab) has the job, plus the two rules that decide whether a deploy lands where your triggers look: the environment name, and the `review/` prefix that marks a preview environment transient.

## Create a trigger

A connected integration does nothing on its own. Create a deployment trigger that matches the deployments you now emit, and name the flows it runs. See [Set up a trigger](/triggers/set-up-a-trigger).

## Verify

Push a commit and let it deploy. Confirm a new run appears in QA Wolf under the expected environment. When none does, [Diagnose a trigger](/triggers/diagnose-a-trigger) says whether the deployment arrived and which condition failed.
